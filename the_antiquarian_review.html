Submission Guidelines

The Antiquarian Review maintains strict university-level standards for all published student work. Please review our criteria before submitting your manuscript.

1. Academic Rigor and Originality

All submissions must represent original historical research. We do not publish standard school essays or unanalyzed summaries of historical events. Your paper must present a distinct thesis and defend it using documented evidence.

2. Primary Source Requirement

A hallmark of our journal is the emphasis on primary documents. Papers must heavily utilize and directly quote from primary sources (e.g., archival letters, treaties, original maps, material artifacts, period-specific newspaper clippings).

3. Formatting and Citations

We strictly adhere to the Chicago Manual of Style (Notes and Bibliography format). All claims must be supported by footnotes. Submissions lacking proper footnotes or a comprehensive bibliography will be returned without review.

The Review Process

Upon submission, your manuscript will undergo a blind peer-review process by our Editorial Board. You will receive structured feedback within 14 days, detailing required revisions or acceptance status.

Material Culture

Preservation and Analysis of Imperial German Militaria: A Methodological Approach

Editor-in-Chief

The Antiquarian Review • Vol I, Issue 1

The physical preservation of historical artifacts offers an unparalleled, tactile connection to the geopolitical realities of the early 20th century. This paper examines the microcrystalline wax restoration techniques applied to a 1914 Iron Cross, contextualizing the artifact within the broader logistical framework of the Imperial German war economy.

By analyzing maker marks and material degradation, we can deduce supply chain limitations on the Western Front. The substitution of brass for lower-quality alloys in later-war productions provides a metallurgical timeline of the British naval blockade's efficacy.
